Meyl, Hively, and Nikolova–Zimmerman

A comparison for classically trained electrical and electronics engineers. What each author claims Maxwell is missing, what “scalar / longitudinal” means in each framework, and how the experiments differ from ordinary RF practice.

Audience assumption. You are comfortable with Maxwell–Heaviside equations, Lorenz/Coulomb gauges, skin depth, Faraday cages, TEM vs TM/TE modes, and the continuity equation. This page does not ask you to accept any of the three programs. It maps them onto that toolkit so you can see where they leave textbook CED and where they overlap each other.

1. Classical baseline (what they all say is incomplete)

In SI Heaviside–Gibbs form, source-free Maxwell theory has \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0\) (no magnetic monopoles) and \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{D}=\rho\), \(\nabla\times\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{J}+\partial\mathbf{D}/\partial t\), \(\nabla\times\mathbf{E}=-\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t\). Taking the divergence of Ampère plus Gauss yields the continuity equation \(\partial\rho/\partial t+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}=0\).

Potentials are introduced by \(\mathbf{B}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}\) and \(\mathbf{E}=-\nabla\Phi-\partial\mathbf{A}/\partial t\). The combination

C ≡ ∇·A + (1/c²) ∂Φ/∂t

is set to zero in the Lorenz gauge. That choice decouples the wave equations for \(\mathbf{A}\) and \(\Phi\) and is usually presented as “just a gauge,” not a physical field. In free space the propagating solutions used in radio are transverse electromagnetic (TEM) waves: \(\mathbf{E}\) and \(\mathbf{H}\) perpendicular to \(\mathbf{k}\), Poynting vector along \(\mathbf{k}\).

A conducting enclosure (Faraday cage) cancels interior fields for wavelengths large compared with apertures and for wall thickness large compared with skin depth \(\delta=\sqrt{2/\omega\mu\sigma}\). That is the EE’s default expectation.

All three authors argue that something physical is lost when \(C=0\) is imposed, when only transverse radiation is admitted, or when \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0\) is treated as an absolute law rather than a constraint that might fail in an extended theory. They do not use the same replacement.

3. Three different “missing terms”

Meyl

Rebuilds field theory from Faraday unipolar induction and a vortex term in the electric field. The “scalar wave” is a longitudinal electric structure that can contract, tunnel, and carry energy without a TEM pair. Magnetic monopoles / magnetic charge are part of the intended symmetry. Language overlaps Tesla, neutrinos, and biological scalar waves.

Hively

Does not throw out Maxwell’s \(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{B}\). Promotes the Lorenz-gauge leftover \(C\) to a physical field. Extended Ampère / Gauss acquire \(\nabla C\) and \(\partial C/\partial t\) terms. Wave equations for \(\mathbf{A}\), \(\Phi\), and \(C\) exist without choosing a gauge. SLW is the \(B=0\), \(C\neq 0\) solution with longitudinal \(\mathbf{E}\).

Zimmerman / Nikolova

Keeps Maxwell but insists the vector potential radiates and can be received when \(\mathbf{B}\) is weak or arranged to be longitudinal-\(\mathbf{A}\) dominated (TM01-like). Detection is a momentum kick on charges (AB-like), not a \(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}\) or TEM \(\mathbf{E}\) on a wire antenna.

4. Equations an EE can compare

Textbook Lorenz gauge (set to zero)

∇·A + (1/c²) ∂Φ/∂t = 0

Hively’s move is: do not set this to zero. Call it \(C(x,t)\) and let it obey a wave equation sourced by the residual of the continuity equation \(\partial\rho/\partial t+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}\). That is why reviewers immediately ask about local charge conservation and Ohm’s law inside copper.

Meyl’s narrative (not the same algebra)

Meyl does not use Hively’s \(C\) as the primary object. He talks of potential vortices derived from Faraday’s law and of a split of the wave equation into transverse Hertzian and longitudinal scalar parts. Critics (e.g. G. W. Bruhn) argue the split is inconsistent with Maxwell and that the resulting “moving scalar wave” is not a solution of the Maxwell system in a charge-neutral homogeneous medium.

Zimmerman’s detection claim

A time-varying longitudinal \(\mathbf{A}\) changes the canonical momentum \(p-q\mathbf{A}\) of charges in a plasma column or biased neon. Reversing detector bias reverses received phase. A conventional monopole in the TEM null (“off the end”) can still couple if the wave is an \(\mathbf{A}\)-wave. Nikolova’s antenna work is the academic near-field / potential-aware backdrop often paired with Zimmerman in later combined models.

5. Faraday cages — three different stories

  • Classical EE: continuous conductor, seams bonded, thickness ≫ δ, holes ≪ λ → high attenuation of TEM.
  • Meyl: in “EM mode” the kit LED goes out inside the box; in “scalar mode” (frequency / offset tweak) it stays on. Interpreted as tunneling vortices, not leakage. Video of the scalar-wave kit transmitting through a Faraday cage / metal box: youtube.com/watch?v=6M8YtCbBXLY (two-cage variant: youtube.com/watch?v=YbRBrnr9nZs).
  • Hively: SLW should not see the same skin-effect exponential. Patent and IARD material show S21 through metal and plots of dB vs log distance. Reviewers ask whether seams, connectors, and common-mode currents explain the residual.
  • Zimmerman: the issue is not mainly “through a box” but “invisible to a TEM antenna.” A plasma/neon aligned with \(\mathbf{k}\) is the receiver. Shielding arguments are secondary.

For a practicing EMC engineer the first test is always: bond the seams, feed with optical isolation or battery power, measure residual slot leakage, and compare against a calibrated TEM illuminator. None of the three programs has become an IEC / MIL-STD shielding method.

6. Magnetic monopoles (why Meyl cites 2009)

Maxwell/Heaviside \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0\) is the statement Meyl says his university would not let him challenge in class. In later talks he says monopoles were “discovered in 2009.” That refers to emergent monopoles in spin ice (Dy₂Ti₂O₇, Ho₂Ti₂O₇):

  • Morris, Tennant et al., Science (online 3 Sep 2009) — Dirac strings in Dy₂Ti₂O₇ (Helmholtz-Zentrum Berlin).
  • Fennell, Bramwell et al., Science (online 3 Sep 2009) — Coulomb phase in Ho₂Ti₂O₇.
  • Bramwell et al., Nature 461, 956 (15 Oct 2009) — monopole charge and current via μSR.

These are quasiparticles at the ends of Dirac strings in a frustrated magnet, not free Dirac monopoles in vacuum. They do not rewrite circuit theory or EMC standards. Hively’s EED and Zimmerman’s \(\mathbf{A}\)-waves do not need this 2009 result. That is a clean discriminator: monopoles are central for Meyl, optional or absent for the other two.

8. Overlap map

All three say: textbook radio is only the transverse, divergenceless-B, Lorenz-gauged sector, and a longitudinal or potential-dominated channel is under-used.

Hively ↔ Zimmerman: closest technically. Both stay near potential formulations, microwave frequencies, and “ordinary antennas are the wrong sensor.” Some later EED / SLW simulators explicitly combine a Hively \(C\)-wave with a Nikolova–Zimmerman longitudinal-\(\mathbf{A}\) picture. Neither builds on Meyl’s vortex books.

Meyl ↔ the other two: shared slogans (scalar waves, Faraday cages fail, Maxwell incomplete) and shared Tesla mythology. Different algebra, different detectors (LED kits vs VNA / plasma), and Meyl’s extra claims (monopoles as fundamental, biology, over-unity) are not part of Hively’s patent or Zimmerman’s JAP experiment.

10. Practical takeaway

If you only remember one distinction:

  • Meyl = Tesla / vortex / monopole program, kit LEDs, cages “transparent” in scalar mode, extra-physics claims.
  • Hively = keep Maxwell fields, promote Lorenz leftover \(C\) to a wave, SLW with longitudinal \(\mathbf{E}\) and weak \(\mathbf{B}\), VNA + metal-wall tests.
  • Nikolova–Zimmerman = longitudinal \(\mathbf{A}\) / TM01 + wrong-sensor story; plasma or biased discharge as the receiver.

An EE evaluating any of them should demand the same things used in EMC and antenna ranges: calibrated field probes, documented seam integrity, isolated power, frequency-by-frequency comparison with a known TEM illuminator, and a model that reduces to Maxwell when the extra field (\(C\), vortex, or free monopole) is set to zero.
