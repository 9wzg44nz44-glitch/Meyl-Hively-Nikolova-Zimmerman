Meyl, Hively, and Nikolova–Zimmerman

A comparison for classically trained electrical and electronics engineers. What each author claims Maxwell is missing, what “scalar / longitudinal” means in each framework, and how the experiments differ from ordinary RF practice.

Audience assumption. You are comfortable with Maxwell–Heaviside equations, Lorenz/Coulomb gauges, skin depth, Faraday cages, TEM vs TM/TE modes, and the continuity equation. This page does not ask you to accept any of the three programs. It maps them onto that toolkit so you can see where they leave textbook CED and where they overlap each other.

1. Classical baseline (what they all say is incomplete)

Textbook electrodynamics in SI Heaviside–Gibbs form is four field equations. An EE uses them every day for antennas, shielding, and circuits.

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{D} = \rho$$

Gauss’s law for electricity: electric flux out of a volume equals the enclosed free charge density $\rho$. $\mathbf{D}=\varepsilon\mathbf{E}$ in linear media.

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$$

Gauss’s law for magnetism: magnetic field lines have no beginning or end. This is the “no magnetic monopoles” statement Meyl challenges.

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$

Faraday’s law: a changing magnetic field produces a circulating electric field. This is the induction law transformers and loops use. Meyl takes a form of this law as his starting axiom.

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J} + \frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$$

Ampère–Maxwell law: current density $\mathbf{J}$ plus displacement current produce a circulating magnetic field. $\mathbf{B}=\mu\mathbf{H}$ in linear media.

Take the divergence of Ampère–Maxwell and substitute Gauss. You recover local charge conservation:

$$\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{J} = 0$$

Continuity equation: charge that leaves a volume must appear as current through the surface. Hively’s reviewers focus here, because his extra scalar field is sourced by any leftover of this identity.

Fields are written from potentials so that the two homogeneous Maxwell equations are automatic:

$$\mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}$$

Magnetic field is the curl of the magnetic vector potential $\mathbf{A}$. Any field of this form automatically satisfies $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0$.

$$\mathbf{E} = -\nabla\Phi - \frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial t}$$

Electric field is minus the gradient of the scalar potential $\Phi$ minus the time derivative of $\mathbf{A}$. The first term is the electrostatic (irrotational) part; the second is the induced (Faraday) part.

Combining those definitions produces the Lorenz-gauge leftover, which textbooks usually set to zero:

$$C \equiv \nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} + \frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}$$

$C$ is a scalar built only from potentials. In the Lorenz gauge one forces $C=0$. That decouples the wave equations for $\mathbf{A}$ and $\Phi$. Hively’s program is to keep $C$ as a real dynamical field instead of a gauge condition.

Free-space radio waves used in communications are transverse electromagnetic (TEM):

$$\mathbf{E}\perp\mathbf{k},\qquad \mathbf{H}\perp\mathbf{k},\qquad \mathbf{S}=\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}\parallel\mathbf{k}$$

$\mathbf{k}$ is the propagation direction. $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{H}$ oscillate across that direction. Power flows along $\mathbf{k}$ via the Poynting vector $\mathbf{S}$. All three authors claim an additional longitudinal or potential-dominated channel is missing from this picture.

A metal wall attenuates a TEM wave over a skin depth

$$\delta = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\omega\mu\sigma}}$$

$\omega$ is radian frequency, $\mu$ permeability, $\sigma$ conductivity. For copper at 8 GHz, $\delta$ is a fraction of a micrometre. A Faraday cage works when walls are many skin depths thick and apertures are much smaller than the wavelength $\lambda$.

3. Three different “missing terms”

Meyl

Rebuilds field theory from Faraday unipolar induction and a vortex term in the electric field. The “scalar wave” is a longitudinal electric structure that can contract, tunnel, and carry energy without a TEM pair. Magnetic monopoles are part of the intended symmetry.

Hively

Does not throw out Maxwell’s $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$. Promotes the Lorenz leftover $C$ to a physical field. Extended Ampère and Gauss acquire $\nabla C$ and $\partial C/\partial t$. SLW is the $\mathbf{B}=0$, $C\neq 0$ solution with longitudinal $\mathbf{E}$.

Zimmerman / Nikolova

Keeps Maxwell but insists $\mathbf{A}$ radiates and can be received when the field is longitudinal-$\mathbf{A}$ dominated (TM01-like). Detection is a momentum kick on charges, not $\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$ on a wire antenna.

4. Equations an EE can compare

4.1 Textbook Lorenz gauge

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} + \frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t} = 0$$

This is $C=0$. It is a convenient choice, not a Maxwell equation. With this choice the potentials each satisfy an ordinary wave equation driven by $\mathbf{J}$ and $\rho$.

4.2 Hively’s extended electrodynamics (EED)

Keep $C$ and add it to the inhomogeneous Maxwell pair. In vacuum-style form used in EED papers:

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E} - \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon} = -\frac{\partial C}{\partial t}$$

Extended Gauss law. The usual $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}=\rho/\varepsilon$ is corrected by $-\partial C/\partial t$. If $C$ varies in time, the electric flux and the charge density need not match the classical way.

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{B} - \mu\varepsilon\frac{\partial\mathbf{E}}{\partial t} - \mu\mathbf{J} = -\nabla C$$

Extended Ampère law. An extra $-\nabla C$ acts like another current. A spatial gradient of $C$ can drive fields even where $\mathbf{J}$ is small.

Taking the divergence of the extended Ampère law and using extended Gauss gives a wave equation for $C$:

$$\nabla^{2}C - \frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}C}{\partial t^{2}} = \mu\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}\right)$$

If charge is exactly conserved, the right-hand side is zero and $C$ still can propagate as a free wave. If there is any local mismatch of continuity, that mismatch sources $C$. This is why reviewers ask whether EED violates charge conservation or Ohm’s law $\mathbf{J}=\sigma\mathbf{E}$ inside a conductor.

Hively’s scalar-longitudinal wave (SLW) is the special solution

$$\mathbf{B}=0,\qquad C\neq 0,\qquad \mathbf{E}\parallel\nabla C$$

No circulating $\mathbf{B}$, so there is no ordinary TEM Poynting pair. The electric field is longitudinal (along the gradient of $C$, hence along the intended propagation). That is the state he says should not see the classical skin-effect exponential in the same way.

4.3 Meyl’s narrative (different algebra)

Meyl does not use Hively’s $C$ as the primary object. He starts from Faraday induction and argues that an electric vortex (potential vortex) is already inside that law. He then splits wave behavior into a transverse Hertzian part and a longitudinal “scalar” part.

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}\quad\text{(Meyl’s preferred axiom)}$$

Same Faraday law an EE already knows. Meyl’s claim is that Heaviside’s later form of Maxwell discarded vortex / magnetic-charge content that Faraday’s original induction picture still allowed.

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = \mu\rho_{m}\quad\text{(what Meyl wants instead of }0\text{)}$$

$\rho_{m}$ would be magnetic charge density. If this were true in free space, magnetic field lines could begin and end. Meyl treats the 2009 spin-ice experiments as support. In textbook CED, $\rho_{m}=0$ everywhere.

Critics (for example G. W. Bruhn) argue that Meyl’s split of the wave equation is not a consistent Maxwell solution in a charge-neutral homogeneous medium, so the moving “scalar wave” is not the same object as Hively’s $C$-wave.

4.4 Zimmerman / Nikolova: longitudinal vector potential

A charge in an electromagnetic field has canonical momentum

$$\boldsymbol{\pi} = \mathbf{p} - q\mathbf{A}$$

$\mathbf{p}$ is mechanical momentum, $q$ is charge, $\mathbf{A}$ is the vector potential. The Aharonov–Bohm effect is the statement that $\mathbf{A}$ can change the phase or momentum of a charge even where $\mathbf{B}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ is locally small. Zimmerman treats a time-harmonic longitudinal $\mathbf{A}$ as a receivable wave.

$$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r},t) \approx A_{\parallel}(\mathbf{r})\,e^{j\omega t}\,\hat{\mathbf{k}}$$

$\mathbf{A}$ oscillates along the propagation axis $\hat{\mathbf{k}}$, not across it. That is the opposite of a TEM wave, where $\mathbf{A}$ (and $\mathbf{E}$) are transverse. A TM01 coax or circular-waveguide launch is used because that mode has an on-axis $E_{z}$ / $A_{z}$ component.

$$\mathbf{F} = q\left(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}\right)$$

The Lorentz force an EE uses on a wire antenna. Zimmerman’s point is that a dipole or loop looking for this TEM force can sit in a null while a plasma column or biased neon still responds to $\partial\mathbf{A}/\partial t$ along its axis. Reversing the detector bias current is said to reverse the received phase.

5. Faraday cages — three different stories

  • Classical EE: continuous conductor, seams bonded, thickness $\gg\delta$, holes $\ll\lambda$ gives high TEM attenuation.
  • Meyl: in “EM mode” the kit LED goes out inside the box; in “scalar mode” (frequency / offset tweak) it stays on. Interpreted as tunneling vortices, not leakage. Video of the scalar-wave kit transmitting through a Faraday cage / metal box: youtube.com/watch?v=6M8YtCbBXLY (two-cage variant: youtube.com/watch?v=YbRBrnr9nZs).
  • Hively: SLW should not see the same $e^{-x/\delta}$ skin-effect factor. Patent and IARD material show $S_{21}$ through metal and plots of dB versus $\log r$. Reviewers ask whether seams, connectors, and common-mode currents explain the residual.
  • Zimmerman: the issue is not mainly “through a box” but “invisible to a TEM antenna.” A plasma or neon aligned with $\mathbf{k}$ is the receiver.

For a practicing EMC engineer the first test is: bond the seams, use optical isolation or battery power, measure residual slot leakage, and compare against a calibrated TEM illuminator. None of the three programs has become an IEC or MIL-STD shielding method.

6. Magnetic monopoles (why Meyl cites 2009)

The textbook identity $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0$ is the statement Meyl says his university would not let him challenge in class. In later talks he says monopoles were “discovered in 2009.” That refers to emergent monopoles in spin ice (Dy2Ti2O7, Ho2Ti2O7):

  • Morris, Tennant et al., Science (online 3 Sep 2009) — Dirac strings in Dy2Ti2O7 (Helmholtz-Zentrum Berlin).
  • Fennell, Bramwell et al., Science (online 3 Sep 2009) — Coulomb phase in Ho2Ti2O7.
  • Bramwell et al., Nature 461, 956 (15 Oct 2009) — monopole charge and current via μSR.

These are quasiparticles at the ends of Dirac strings in a frustrated magnet, not free Dirac monopoles in vacuum. They do not rewrite circuit theory or EMC standards. Hively’s EED and Zimmerman’s $\mathbf{A}$-waves do not need this 2009 result. That is a clean discriminator: monopoles are central for Meyl, optional or absent for the other two.

8. Overlap map

All three say textbook radio is only the transverse, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0$, Lorenz-gauged sector, and a longitudinal or potential-dominated channel is under-used.

Hively ↔ Zimmerman: closest technically. Both stay near potential formulations, microwave frequencies, and “ordinary antennas are the wrong sensor.” Some later EED / SLW simulators combine a Hively $C$-wave with a Nikolova–Zimmerman longitudinal-$\mathbf{A}$ picture. Neither builds on Meyl’s vortex books.

Meyl ↔ the other two: shared slogans (scalar waves, Faraday cages fail, Maxwell incomplete) and shared Tesla language. Different algebra, different detectors (LED kits versus VNA or plasma), and Meyl’s extra claims (fundamental monopoles, biology, over-unity) are not part of Hively’s patent or Zimmerman’s JAP experiment.

10. Practical takeaway

  • Meyl = Tesla / vortex / monopole program, kit LEDs, cages “transparent” in scalar mode.
  • Hively = keep Maxwell fields, promote Lorenz leftover $C$ to a wave, SLW with longitudinal $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}\approx 0$.
  • Nikolova–Zimmerman = longitudinal $\mathbf{A}$ / TM01; plasma or neon as the sensor, not a TEM dipole.

An EE evaluating any of them should demand the same things used in EMC and antenna ranges: calibrated field probes, documented seam integrity, isolated power, frequency-by-frequency comparison with a known TEM illuminator, and a model that reduces to Maxwell when the extra field ($C$, vortex, or free monopole) is set to zero.
